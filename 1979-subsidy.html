1979 Mission — Phase 1 (136 units): Illustrative 15‑Year Subsidy Requirement

  Recently at the 1979 Mission st. project, construction began on phase 1 of the project for 136 units of affordable that will be renting under guidelines established by government for capping rents and renting only to families with incomes falling between .3 and .5 of Area Median Income as it appears in regulations for housing finances. No proforma for 20 year cash flows has yet been published for phase 1 but MOHCD eventually publishes the cash flow pro-forma as it has  in past - such reports are available for comparable public projects such as 1515 South van Ness, 1633 Valencia street , Potrero block b.  Use the following available data to summarize PUPA subsidy amounts that will be required over the next 15 years to fill the cash flow gap ( annual gross rents - amortized repayments of total upfront development costs ) . your figures don't need to be exact but should convey the general size of the ongoing commitment that often falls on MOHCD and LOSP programs that are getting harder to maintain with construction costs rising so much faster than average income. 
1979 phase 1 data:
- 136 units 
- rents capped in range (.3 to .5 AMI ) 
- total construction cost minus land acquisition 144 million USD
- per unit land acquisition based on Terner center average for SF public housing $120,000
- 15 year operations period in which rents increase by .025 per year

Caveat up front: No MOHCD pro‑forma exists yet for Phase 1, so everything below is a benchmark model built off the published 20‑year cash‑flow pro‑formas for comparable deals (1515 South Van Ness, 1633 Valencia, Potrero Block B / Rebuild Potrero, Casa Adelante 2060 Folsom). Figures are rounded and meant to convey order of magnitude, not audit-grade precision.

4. How MOHCD Actually Books This (Two Very Different Numbers)

The $57K–$76K PUPA above is the economically honest number, but it is not what shows up as a recurring line item, because SF converts almost all of it into a one‑time capital soft loan. Reconciling:

Debt capacity test (Yr 1): - EGI $2,140,000 − Opex $2,000,000 = NOI ≈ $140,000 (~$1,030/unit) - At 1.15x DCR → supportable hard debt service ~$122,000 → permanent loan of roughly $1.8–2.0M - That is ~1.2% of a $160M project. At 30–50% AMI, the rents essentially support no conventional debt.

Implied sources:

Source Amount Per Unit
4% LIHTC equity (130% DDA boost, ~$0.82/credit) ~$55M $404,000
Permanent conventional loan ~$2M $15,000
State (AHSC/MHP/IIG), AHP, other ~$15M $110,000
Deferred developer fee ~$5M $37,000

So the commitment appears as ~$600K–$700K/unit of upfront local capital subsidy, structured as residual‑receipts soft debt that the published pro‑formas for 1515 SVN, 1633 Valencia and Potrero B show recovering only single‑digit percentages of principal over 20 years. Spread over 15 years undiscounted, that $83M is ~$40,700 PUPA; at a 4% public cost of capital, ~$55,000 PUPA.

6. Why This Keeps Getting Worse

  1. The scissors in every MOHCD pro‑forma. Standard trending is 2.5% income growth vs. 3.5% expense growth. Restricted rents are capped by AMI (which has grown ~2–3%/yr), while insurance, water/sewer, and on‑site labor have run well above 3.5%. Cash flow crosses to negative around year 8 in this model — earlier than the year 12–15 crossovers shown in the older 1633 Valencia / Potrero B pro‑formas, which is exactly the trend MOHCD asset management is now flagging.

  2. Cost per unit vs. income per unit. SF affordable production ran roughly $450–500K/unit in 2012 and is now ~$1.1–1.2M — about 7–8%/yr. AMI has grown ~2–3%/yr. The subsidy-per-unit gap therefore compounds at ~4–5%/yr in real terms. That single divergence, not any operating inefficiency, is what drives the $600K+/unit local gap.

  3. Deep targeting is the multiplier. At 30–50% AMI the rents support essentially zero hard debt, so ~99% of the stack must be equity or grant. A project at 50–80% AMI could carry $15–25M of permanent debt on the same building — meaning the choice to serve 30–50% AMI is itself worth ~$100K–$180K/unit in additional required subsidy.

  4. LIHTC equity is the only elastic source, and it’s price-sensitive. A 10‑cent move in 4% credit pricing on this deal is ~$7M — roughly $51,000/unit of additional local gap, or another ~$3,400 PUPA over 15 years.

Bottom line for Phase 1: expect MOHCD’s eventual pro‑forma to show roughly $600K–$700K per unit of one-time local gap financing (~$80–95M) plus a recurring operating/LOSP commitment starting near $5,000–$6,000 PUPA and growing past $10,000 PUPA by year 15. Stated as the true all-in annualized cost that the public sector is absorbing, the number is on the order of $55,000–$75,000 per unit per year.
